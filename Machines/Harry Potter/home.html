Pick your machine above, then a rulesheet section. The artwork changes to match. The playfield highlights the shots for the step you are on, and the arrow keys move between steps.

The rulesheet does not separate the editions. Its only edition note is a pair of layout photos, one of the Collector's Edition and one of the Arcade Edition, which show the same playfield layout. Reviews also list a Wizard's Edition, which this guide does not show separately.

The rulesheet is for code 1.26. Most of the lesson shot tables are incomplete, Years 5 and 6 list lesson names only, and the Voldemort battle in The Boy Who Lived is not described. In a few places it gives two different rules. The guide marks those gaps instead of filling them in.

How far to trust the highlights
  • Rules come from the supplied rulesheet, based on code 1.26. Its Layout section is two photos with no text, so the shot names on the playfield are my reading of the printed inserts and of the rulesheet's descriptions.
  • Each highlighted feature carries a tag. Clear means a printed label, insert or toy identifies it in the photo. Estimated means I placed it from context and it is worth confirming on the machine.
  • The Quidditch inserts fix the left ramp, the Explore Hogwarts location inserts fix the center ramp, the house inserts and the staircase fix the right ramp, and the Diagon Alley shop inserts fix the center lane. The Death Eater, the staircase, the cauldron, the Whomping Willow, the upper playfield, the year, horcrux and HARRY inserts and the upper flipper are clear.
  • The library ramp, inner loop and right orbit are my reading of the remaining Advance Term arrows and may be swapped. Paths past every entrance are estimated. The scoop, the hole under the upper flipper, the right spinner, the Death Eater drop target, the Hagrid, RON, Hermione and allies targets, the wand lock and the pop bumpers are estimated. The Y lane, the left and upper spinners, the upper playfield target and the 24 snitch inserts are not marked.
  • The Collector's Edition photo is Jersey Jack Pinball's own top view. It has gold wire ramps and a gold apron.
  • The Arcade Edition photo is P1-AG's top view, aligned to the Collector's Edition photo. It has chrome wire ramps and a different apron; the playfield layout is the same.
