Pick your machine above, then a rulesheet section. The artwork changes to match. The playfield highlights the shots for the step you are on, and the arrow keys move between steps.

The rulesheet treats the three models as one game, with one rules note: Love Gun Multiball adds its third ball differently on the Pro. Stern's features list shows hardware differences too (drop targets, a Starchild drop target and a Demon ball lock on the Premium and LE), so the Pro and Premium each have their own photo. The LE uses the Premium photo.

How far to trust the highlights
  • Rules come from the community rulesheet, based on code 1.40.0. It has no Layout section, and two modes are only partly documented.
  • Shot positions come from Stern's service manual. Its lamp list names an RGB arrow for each of the six main shots (LT Orbit, Starchild, LT Ramp, Demon, RT Ramp, RT Orbit) and its diagrams show where the switches sit. I matched the diagrams to the photo by eye, so treat every lane as approximate.
  • Each highlighted feature carries a tag. Clear means a printed label, insert or toy identifies it in the photo. Estimated means I placed it from the manual or from context and it is worth confirming on the machine.
  • The rulesheet and the manual use different names. I read the rulesheet's center ramp as the manual's LT Ramp (Stern's features list: left plastic ramp feeding the left wire ramp), the left loop as the LT Orbit, the STAR targets shot as the Starchild arrow, and the right loop as the RT Orbit. These are my readings.
  • The KISS and ARMY target banks, the green lock targets, the STAR targets and the spinner are hard to see from above; they are placed from the manual.
  • The photos come from Stern's 2015 sales flyers. Round callout pictures on the flyer cover a little of the left and right edges of the playfield.
  • On the Premium and LE, the KISS bank is drop targets rather than standups, a Starchild drop target with a captive ball sits in the Starchild area, an amplifier stands at the upper left, and the Demon head holds a three-ball lock. The LE flyer uses the same playfield photo as the Premium.

Sources: Rulesheet, Stern Pro manual, Stern Premium manual, Stern features list. The photos are from Stern's 2015 Pro and Premium flyers, saved by the Wayback Machine.
